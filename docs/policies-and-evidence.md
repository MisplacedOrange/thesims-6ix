# Policies and evidence

## Reading the evidence

The review cutoff is October 17, 2026 in Toronto. A primary source can verify that an organization announced a program without verifying its eventual results. Values on undated live pages are provisional context until their publication vintage is established. ## Municipal grocery stores

Council adopted amended item 2026.MM39.27 on March 25 and 26, 2026. Anthony Perruzza proposed it and Mike Colle seconded it. It calls for four stores, one per Community Council district, priority for low-income areas with poor grocery access, a City not-for-profit model, possible fee relief, pooled purchasing, and local or regional sourcing. Staff must return with a vision and strategy in Q2 2027. The decision supplies no store addresses or opening dates. [Adopted Council decision](https://secure.toronto.ca/council/agenda-item.do?item=2026.MM39.27).

The contemporaneous financial review says the 2026 operating budget and capital plan contained no establishment or operating funds for the stores. That is a dated funding assessment, not proof that no later decision exists. [March financial review, page 9](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/rm/bgrd/backgroundfile-285659.pdf).

Sahar Raza's Daily Bread submission supports exploration and requests feasibility and financial analysis. It argues that removing thin retail margins alone constrains possible savings. Treat this as a challenge the model must test through explicit costs and subsidies. [Daily Bread submission](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/mm/comm/communicationfile-206612.pdf).

The scenario varies site, supplier terms, rent, staffing, retail price, subsidy, opening date, and allocation. Report fixed-basket savings, access, unmet demand, operating deficit, fiscal cost, and effects on existing retailers. Distinguish lower prices paid by households from lower total resource costs.

## Surplus rescue and processing

Second Harvest announced the Etobicoke facility on September 15, 2026, with first production expected later that month. It states up to 750,000 pounds of fresh produce annually and planned prepared-fruit and vegetable supply for 28,000 students through TFSS. Its social-enterprise model includes purchasing farm produce as well as processing suitable rescued surplus. Capacity and intended student reach are not audited throughput or delivered meals. [Facility announcement](https://www.secondharvest.ca/post/second-harvest-new-food-processing-facility).

Model purchased and rescued inputs separately. Limit throughput by actual shifts, eligible supply, recipes, storage, equipment, and demand. Test direct redistribution against processing, including transport and spoilage. Do not assign all announced facility output to rescued food or assume every portion replaces an entire meal.

## School meals and shared purchasing

The City's February 24 announcement states an expansion to 155 additional schools and 62,000 students. It announces CAD 1.4 million from Bloomberg Philanthropies and a FoodReach partnership for a School Food Hub. The City's potential family savings figure is an estimate, not an observed household outcome. [City announcement](https://www.toronto.ca/news/torontos-school-food-program-wins-1-4-million-prize-as-city-tackles-affordability/).

The final Toronto Public Health budget includes CAD 6 million for expansion, with up to 77 school communities in spring and 78 in fall. These are planned phases. Annual student reach, daily participants, portions, and full meals require separate fields. [Final TPH budget, pages 5, 15, and 24](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/86ce-2026-Public-Book-TPH-V1.pdf).

Test meal expansion separately from procurement pooling. Then combine them. Record uptake, delivered cost, kitchen capacity, waste, and changes in household purchases. The 28,000 students in the processing announcement may overlap with school expansion. Never add the groups without linked, deduplicated program data.

## Meituan-style delivery

The user confirmed Meituan-style food delivery. Meituan's January 2026 company release reports 65 routes and more than 740,000 commercial orders by November 2025. Its Shanghai West Bund announcement describes a network with receiving equipment. These establish an operating reference abroad, not Toronto unit costs or achievable delivery times. [Meituan January release](https://www.meituan.com/news/NN260106200001758), [Shanghai release](https://www.meituan.com/news/NN260911177010407).

Use hub-to-locker routes and include the customer's pickup trip. Dense Toronto deployment remains hypothetical. Transport Canada's lower-risk BVLOS category has airspace and location restrictions and does not itself establish eligibility for an urban route. A real deployment needs route-specific assessment, applicable permissions, operator readiness, and site agreements. [Transport Canada changes](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/2025-summary-changes-canada-drone-regulations), [where to fly](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/learn-rules-you-fly-your-drone/where-fly-your-drone).

Compare against ground delivery at equal demand and service standards. Test weather downtime, utilization, loading, battery reserve, handoff, maintenance, site cost, accessibility, and failed orders. Noise and emissions are proxies until supported by measured vehicle and route data.

## What existing impact research can support

The Competition Bureau's grocery study distinguishes food gross margins from broader corporate measures and discusses entry barriers. Use it to avoid treating gross margin as removable net profit. It does not supply a municipal-store savings coefficient. [Competition Bureau study](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/canada-needs-more-grocery-competition).

A 2024 Scotland study found higher free-meal uptake without economically meaningful attendance gains. This is a reason to measure uptake and household effects separately from attendance, rather than hard-code an educational benefit. Its effect sizes do not automatically transfer to Toronto. [Borbely and colleagues](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/90936/).

TFSS's local research index includes an evaluation of seven sites. It can inform outcomes and implementation questions. The first simulator does not treat that evaluation as proof of citywide causal effects. [TFSS research](https://tfss.ca/research/).

No audited post-launch evaluation of these 2026 initiatives was established in this review. The plan therefore tests their mechanisms and specifies how to learn from observed rollout. It does not claim measured Toronto savings or public sentiment.

## Real-world implementation estimates

The ranges below are design-team planning assumptions for scenario analysis. They are not official schedules or sourced forecasts. Start the clock at the stated trigger, then vary delays.

| Intervention | Illustrative time from trigger | Dependencies and delayed case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First municipal grocery site | 9 to 18 months after strategy, funding, operating model, and suitable site are approved | Lease, procurement, fit-out, refrigeration, food-premise readiness, hiring, contracts. Test 24+ months or no launch. Q2 2027 is a strategy deadline. |
| Existing processor ramp | 1 to 6 months after verified commissioning and supply contracts | Staff, recipes, cold chain, buyers, and distribution. Test low utilization or prolonged ramp. Building another facility is a separate 12 to 24+ month assumption. |
| Additional school provision | 3 to 9 months after funding and school readiness | Equipment, staffing, menus, grant approvals, calendar. A missed term can defer service to the next school period. |
| Procurement hub pilot | 6 to 12 months after governance, site, and participants are agreed | Contracts, ordering, storage, cost allocation, delivery. Test 18+ months or partial participation. |
| Toronto drone pilot | No defensible committed opening date | For study only, test a conditional 12 to 24+ month readiness window after a viable route concept. Permission may not arrive. Include a no-approval branch. |

Estimate actual durations through a dependency schedule and operator input. Do not turn the illustrative midpoint into a fact. Software delivery dates appear separately in [delivery](delivery.md).
